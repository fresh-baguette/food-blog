Soft Aloha-Style Dinner Rolls

Soft, barely sweet, pillowy pull-apart dinner rolls: my lighter take on an Aloha-style bun, with a little sourdough discard and Greek yoghurt for flavour and tenderness.

Category Bread
Yield About 24 small rolls
Time Rising + 25–35 min baking

Ingredients

  • 200 g sourdough starter discard (100% hydration)
  • 600 g bread flour
  • 100 g whole-wheat flour
  • 240 ml skim milk
  • 160 g plain 0% Greek yoghurt
  • 2 eggs
  • 30 g sugar
  • 30 g olive oil or neutral oil
  • 14 g salt
  • 10 g instant yeast
  • A little milk, for brushing

Method

  1. Mix the starter discard, milk, yoghurt, eggs, sugar and oil. Add the flours, instant yeast and salt, and combine into a soft dough.
  2. Rest for about 15 minutes, then knead for 8–10 minutes until elastic and relatively smooth. The dough should remain soft, not dry or heavily floured.
  3. Cover and let rise until roughly doubled. Divide and shape into smooth, tightly rounded balls, pinching the seams underneath.
  4. Lightly grease a rectangular baking dish. Arrange the rolls with small gaps between them so they can expand and eventually touch. Cover and proof for about 45–90 minutes, until noticeably puffy.
  5. Brush gently with milk. Bake at 350°F (175°C) until golden and baked through, usually 25–35 minutes for a closely packed stoneware dish. Start checking at 25 minutes and tent loosely with foil if the tops brown too quickly.
  6. Cool in the dish for 5–10 minutes, then move to a rack. Cover loosely with a clean towel while cooling if you prefer a soft crust.

Shaping and yield

This is the larger dough formula I used. It makes approximately two dozen 60 g rolls, depending on how the dough is divided. The photographed Staub dish holds twelve smaller rolls, roughly 50 g of dough apiece; the rest of the batch can be baked separately or used for other soft bread shapes.

Notes

These are Aloha-style rather than traditional Hawaiian sweet rolls: soft and slightly sweet, but deliberately lighter, with Greek yoghurt, skim milk and a little whole wheat instead of a heavily enriched dough. The sourdough discard contributes flavour; the added yeast provides a dependable rise. Their most satisfying feature is the pull-apart crumb, especially while still slightly warm.
